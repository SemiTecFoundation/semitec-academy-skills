# Document Structure — Diploma / Thesis / Research Project

Section-by-section guidance. Each section is marked **Required** or **Optional**,
with what it contains, typical length, and how it scales across work types
(**D** = diploma, **T** = thesis, **R** = research project).

Legend for scale: ● full treatment · ◐ brief · ○ usually omitted.

---

## Front matter

### Title page — Required (D ● / T ● / R ●)
Project title; author full name; supervisor(s) and co-supervisor(s); institution,
faculty/department; degree and work type; city and year. Optional: student ID,
program, logo.

### Abstract — Required (D ● / T ● / R ●)
150–300 words: problem, approach, main results, and contribution. One paragraph,
no citations, self-contained. Provide in the required language(s) (add a second
language version if the institution requires it).

### Keywords — Required (D ● / T ● / R ●)
3–7 terms.

### Acknowledgements — Optional (D ◐ / T ◐ / R ○)
Brief thanks to supervisor, institution, collaborators.

### Table of contents — Required (auto-generated)
Plus **List of Figures**, **List of Tables**, and **List of Abbreviations** when
the document has several of each.

---

## Body

### 1. Introduction — Required (D ● / T ● / R ●)
Background and context; **problem statement**; motivation and relevance;
**objectives / research questions** (and hypotheses if applicable); scope and
limitations; a short outline of the document. Typical length: 2–4 pages.

### 2. Background / Literature Review — Required (D ◐ / T ● / R ◐)
Key concepts and definitions; **related work / state of the art**; how existing
approaches fall short and where this project fits. Thesis: a thorough review;
diploma/research project: a focused summary of the most relevant work.

### 3. Methodology / Approach — Required (D ● / T ● / R ●)
Methods, materials, tools, datasets; the research or engineering approach; why it
was chosen; evaluation plan (metrics, baselines). Enough detail to reproduce the
work.

### 4. Design / Implementation — Required for engineering work (D ● / T ● / R ◐)
System or study **design** with diagrams (architecture, data model, flowcharts);
**implementation** details, key decisions, and challenges. Use figures liberally;
put long code/config in an appendix.

### 5. Results / Experiments / Analysis — Required (D ● / T ● / R ●)
What was produced or measured; experiments and their setup; **results in tables
and figures**; analysis and interpretation. Report honestly, including negative or
inconclusive results.

### 6. Discussion — Optional (D ◐ / T ● / R ◐)
What the results mean; comparison to related work; strengths, weaknesses, threats
to validity. May be merged into Results for shorter works.

### 7. Conclusion & Future Work — Required (D ● / T ● / R ●)
Summary of contributions against the stated objectives; limitations; concrete
future directions. 1–2 pages. No new results here.

---

## Back matter

### References / Bibliography — Required (D ● / T ● / R ●)
All cited sources in a consistent citation style (see
`formatting-and-figures.md`). Every reference is cited in the text; every in-text
citation appears here.

### Appendices — Optional (D ◐ / T ◐ / R ◐)
Supplementary material: full code listings, extra tables/plots, questionnaires,
raw data, user manuals. Label A, B, C…

---

## Suggested minimum by work type

- **Research project (R):** Title, Abstract, Introduction, Methodology, Results,
  Conclusion, References.
- **Diploma (D):** the above **plus** Background and Design/Implementation.
- **Thesis (T):** all sections, with a full Literature Review and Discussion.

Adjust to the author's institution — section names and required set vary.
